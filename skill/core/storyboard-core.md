# Storyboard Core

Status: `FORMALIZED_PRODUCTION_CANDIDATE`.

These five invariants apply across genres and scene types. Scene-specific methods belong to routed capabilities; model syntax, QA, workflow, projects, and historical fixes do not belong here.

## SC1 — Source fidelity and complete coverage

The current script and explicit user revisions are the only story authority. Cover every necessary event, exact dialogue, information change, relationship change, and required end state. Assign each required beat to at least one Shot. Do not invent identity, motive, relationship, location, outcome, prop state, dialogue, or off-screen event.

## SC2 — Perceptible causality, reaction, and reveal

Important stimulus, perception, action, reaction, result, and reveal must occur in an order the audience can perceive. A required result cannot exist only in notes, and withheld information cannot become visible or audible before its trigger. This does not require a dedicated reaction Shot or fixed cutting pattern.

## SC3 — Functional Shots with proportionate complexity

Every Shot must add or transform story, emotion, relationship, space, action, reveal, or transition information. Camera, composition, and cutting complexity must be justified by that function and actual risk. Static Shots are valid; novelty, Shot quotas, scale variety, and camera motion are not goals by themselves.

## SC4 — Internally executable space, time, action, and sound

Subject placement, framing, camera, blocking, action, dialogue, sound, and duration must form one internally consistent and temporally executable Shot within the story-world rules, declared spatial relations, and target generation capability. This does not require real-world physics for fantasy or supernatural events. Use the minimum sufficient spatial claim and never hide an impossible path, action, lip-sync, or duration behind generic wording.

## SC5 — Continuous state and stable identity

Across Shots and connected Scenes, preserve established character identity and the state of people, props, positions, directions, action phases, environment, and VFX subjects. Every change requires a script source or an explicit Shot occurrence. Scene end state records a boundary result; Shot delta records only a change assigned to that Shot and is never inferred from a later end state.

## Explicit exclusions

No project answer, genre package, Seedance prose, fixed 11-module layout, approval/hash/seal state, QA verdict, sample, full-camera tutorial, mandatory ten-field composition, or historical bug catalog is Core authority.
